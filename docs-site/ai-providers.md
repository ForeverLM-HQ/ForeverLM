# AI Providers & Models

**AI runs on your own OpenRouter API key.** Create one at [openrouter.ai/keys](https://openrouter.ai/keys) and add it under **API keys** in the OpenRouter button at the bottom of the sidebar. OpenRouter bills every model at its own cost, and ForeverLM adds nothing on top. OpenRouter is the only supported provider today. **AI is never part of ForeverLM's price**: no credits, no tier, and every model OpenRouter serves. Pay models at cost, or use free models and pay nothing. You pick a provider and model for each AI capability in **Settings**, and the **Activity** list under the sidebar's OpenRouter button shows what each request cost, precise below a cent.

Every text model, whether Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI or the long tail, is served through [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai), on your key, straight from your device to OpenRouter: ForeverLM's servers never see it. A request never falls back silently to another route.

You can also skip the built-in AI entirely and drive ForeverLM from Claude, Codex, Gemini, or Grok, [connected over MCP](/setup-mcp). Connecting an assistant is part of the app, with nothing extra to buy, and ForeverLM charges nothing per MCP tool call: your assistant's subscription covers the thinking. Sources, Schedule, and semantic search work either way. Most people mix the two.

## Chat and reviews (text models)

In-app Chat and reviews use the Default Model chosen in **Settings → Chat** (or in the **Set up ForeverLM** guide). Until you choose one it is Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5. Background tasks (like paper metadata extraction) run on the two Background models in **Settings → Background**: **Text-only**, and **Multimodal** for the work that looks at a figure or a slide. Any one task can have a model of its own on the **Tasks** tab; until you give it one it follows the Background model of its kind. The chat can change them too (`set_ai_task_settings`).

**Settings → Chat** starts with a **Suggested** list: one model from each maker (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral, Meta, NVIDIA and others), the top model that is still affordable, shown as the newest release of its family that OpenRouter lists, so a new release takes its place without an app update. Under it are OpenRouter's **Free** models, then **All models on OpenRouter**: every model OpenRouter serves, as a table you can search and sort, or narrow to free or discounted models. Each column's header has its own filter behind a funnel: the Model column by what a model can do or whether it is new, Maker by maker, Price by price band, Context and Speed by a minimum, Provider by whether you chose one. Models OpenRouter listed in the past week are marked **New**, and the refresh button beside the title reads OpenRouter's list again and says which models are new. A row's check offers the model in the chat's model picker, and its menu makes it the Default Model or a backup. **Settings → Background** lists its models the same way, a price tier down.

Notes:

- The long-tail models (DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen, GLM, GPT OSS, Mistral, Llama, and Nemotron) are served through OpenRouter like every other text model. The picker groups them by the lab that trained them, and you can add any other compatible model from OpenRouter's directory.
- DeepSeek and Kimi are offered only through OpenRouter's US-routed inference; the labs' own APIs are deliberately not integrated.
- **Don't show models that train on my data** (Settings → Data & Privacy; Settings on iPhone) takes the models that train on what you send them out of every model picker, and routes every OpenRouter request only to hosts that neither retain prompts nor train on them, using OpenRouter's `data_collection: deny` provider preference. One model is priced for training: Meta's Muse Spark 1.2 Contributor, whose discount is paid in training consent. The app does not label it; the switch hides it. A model with no compliant host is refused with a message that names the switch. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google's paid API and xAI do not train on API traffic under their own terms, which the switch does not change.
- A model is admitted only while OpenRouter publishes its price, and every charge is the cost OpenRouter reports for that request. A model it cannot price is refused instead of being estimated.
- **Speed, priced per tier.** On the OpenRouter route the chat composer's model menu offers **Speed: Standard / Fast** for a model whose lab publishes a fast-processing endpoint on OpenRouter (`openai/fast`, `anthropic/fast`). Each speed is listed with its own input / output price from that endpoint — Fast is typically double — and a Fast request names the endpoint explicitly with fallbacks off, so it is never quietly served at another tier; OpenRouter's receipt for the endpoint that ran is the cost recorded. A Standard request names no endpoint, so when the lab's endpoint refuses it (a 429 from Google AI Studio, say) OpenRouter moves it to the same model's other standard endpoint rather than failing the chat; tier endpoints are never part of that fallback. A bolt on the model chip shows Fast is on. Models with no fast endpoint show no speed control.
- **Promotions are named, not applied twice.** When OpenRouter is running a promotion on a model's endpoint, the price in the composer menu already reflects it and the subtitle says so ("$2 in / $10 out · 50% off"). Settings → Chat shows the same label on the model's row.
- The chat's model picker shows only the models you offer; turn off the ones you never use in **Settings → Chat**.
- Each provider remembers its own model choice, so switching providers doesn't reset your selection.

## Text to Speech (text-to-speech)

[Text to Speech](/text-to-speech) offers every speech model OpenRouter serves ([openrouter.ai/models?output_modalities=speech](https://openrouter.ai/models?output_modalities=speech)). The Mac and the iPhone read that list live, so a model OpenRouter adds appears without an app update, with the voices OpenRouter lists for it. Every request goes to OpenRouter, Grok and Gemini included, except the OpenAI voice (GPT Realtime 2.1 Mini), which OpenRouter does not serve and ForeverLM reads on your own OpenAI key (it needs one stored, as voice mode does). Everything else goes straight to OpenRouter on your key. Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS with Kore remains the default, and it is also the widest-reaching entry: Google publishes more than 70 languages for it, from Afrikaans and Arabic through Mandarin, Swahili, Tamil, and Vietnamese. The models ForeverLM curates declare their languages. Others are marked as not published, never assumed to be English. Orpheus 3B is left out because it resets its intonation every ~180 characters.

Each model shows OpenRouter's price in the unit OpenRouter bills it by, for the host it routes the model to. Most are per million characters, Fish Audio per million UTF-8 bytes, Gemini per million text and audio tokens, and Seed Audio per minute of audio. The free `:free` models show as free. None of these prices is written into the app, and a model OpenRouter has not priced shows **Price unknown**. The free routes have no production latency or availability guarantee. The exact cost OpenRouter reports, rather than the picker's price, is the record.

On-device macOS speech and a local Qwen3-TTS server remain available without cloud inference cost.

## Voice input (speech-to-text)

Voice dictation in Chat on the Mac uses the model chosen in **Settings → Voice → Speech to text**. The recording is transcribed after you stop, by any transcription model OpenRouter serves ([openrouter.ai/models?output_modalities=transcription](https://openrouter.ai/models?output_modalities=transcription)). The list is read live and grouped by maker, and GPT-4o mini Transcribe is the default. Meeting recordings and podcast episodes use the same list (`speech_to_text.recordings_model`, set from the chat). Each model shows OpenRouter's price per hour of audio, or per million tokens for a model billed by tokens (the GPT-4o transcribe models, Gemini 3.5 Transcribe). Every transcription request goes to OpenRouter.

Audio is sent only to the model you chose, through OpenRouter, and is not kept after transcription. 

On the iPhone, the composer's microphone and spoken reviews use Apple's on-device speech recognition. It is free, works offline, and is not a model you pick.

## Voice mode

[Voice mode](/voice-mode) (talking about a source, the voice assistant, CarPlay) is the one feature that does not go through OpenRouter: it runs on OpenAI's realtime voice model (gpt-realtime-2.1), which OpenRouter does not serve, so it runs on your own OpenAI key and OpenAI bills you for it. See [Your own OpenAI key](/voice-mode#your-own-openai-key). AI Usage lists each conversation with a **BYOK** capsule, since it runs on your own OpenAI key rather than through OpenRouter.

## Semantic search

Semantic source search runs entirely on-device using Apple's NaturalLanguage contextual embeddings. It costs nothing and sends nothing over the network.

## Costs

- **Your key pays, at OpenRouter's cost.** With an OpenRouter key stored under **API keys** in the OpenRouter button at the bottom of the sidebar, everything ForeverLM sends through OpenRouter goes on your key and OpenRouter bills you directly. ForeverLM adds nothing to it. The key covers text models (chat, reviews, and background tasks) *and* every voice, speech-to-text, image and video model OpenRouter serves: the Gemini and Grok voices, GPT-4o mini transcription, the GPT Image, Gemini, FLUX, Recraft and Seedream image models, and Veo, Kling, Wan, Sora and the other video models. **Settings → AI Models → Images** and **→ Videos** list every one of them, read from OpenRouter each time the pane opens, so a model OpenRouter adds shows up there on its own; each card shows OpenRouter's price. Over MCP the same setting is `get_own_router_key` / `set_own_router_key`.
- **Free API credits that come with a subscription can pay for it.** Some paid plans now include monthly API credits, separate from the chat subscription itself: Claude's Max, Ultra and Team plans ([how to claim them](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)); ChatGPT's Pro and Business plans (claim them on your [OpenAI billing page](https://platform.openai.com/settings/organization/billing/overview)); and Google AI Pro, Google One AI Premium (Gemini Advanced), or Google Cloud paid plans, which include monthly API credits or developer quotas in [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey). Each provider asks you to link a billing project or API organization to your plan, and credits typically refresh or expire each billing cycle if unused. Plans change, so check your own account's billing page for what yours includes. To spend the credits through ForeverLM, create an API key in that account (OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google AI Studio), then add it to your OpenRouter account under [Settings → Integrations](https://openrouter.ai/settings/integrations) (OpenRouter calls this *bring your own key*, see its [guide](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/overview/auth/byok)). Select **Always use this key** (and **Never use shared capacity**). Claude, GPT, and Gemini models then run on your own subscription credits, and your OpenRouter key in ForeverLM stays the same.
- **Your key never touches ForeverLM's servers.** It is saved only in your Keychain, which iCloud Keychain syncs end-to-end encrypted to your Macs and iPhone, and the app sends it only to OpenRouter. Connect OpenRouter signs in at openrouter.ai and hands the key back to the app on your Mac directly. The remote ForeverLM connector refuses to carry a key, so an AI assistant cannot set one through it; paste the key into the app instead.
- **The app is free.** ForeverLM costs nothing and sells nothing: your key runs every model, billed by OpenRouter, and OpenRouter's free models cost nothing.
- The **Activity** list under the sidebar's OpenRouter button itemises every request with the exact cost OpenRouter reports, precise below a cent. Models without a published rate are shown as unpriced rather than guessed.
- **ForeverLM pays for no AI.** There is no AI balance, top-up, spending limit or shared plan: every request runs on your own key and is billed by OpenRouter (or, for voice, OpenAI). Someone who wants to cover another person's AI gives them a key to set up on their own devices.
- Background tasks (paper metadata extraction, transcript cleanup, search embeddings) run on the same key and are itemised per task in the ledger. They run on the Background models in **Settings → Background**, which the chat can change too (`set_ai_task_settings`), so a cheap or free model there keeps them cheap. While the Text model is free, meeting work stays on a paid model unless you let it use the free one: a meeting's transcript holds private conversations. Each Background model can also list up to ten **backups**, tried in order when it is busy, fails or sends back nothing usable: right-click a model and choose **Use as Backup**. A backup is billed at its own price. A free model you pick brings the next two free models as its backups when it has none. The chat's model can list backups too, in **Settings → AI Models → Chat**, but only a scheduled task's run moves to them: a chat you type in stays on the model you picked.
- If a provider or model can't complete a request, ForeverLM surfaces the error — it never falls back to a model that is not one of the setting's listed backups, or to heuristic grading.
