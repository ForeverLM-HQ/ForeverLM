# Web Library

> The web app is being rebuilt around notebooks and is not open to everyone
> yet. Use the [ForeverLM Mac app](https://foreverlm.com/download).

Sign in with the Apple Account that you use for ForeverLM. Web Studio reads and
writes the same CloudKit records as the Mac and iPhone apps: your own library,
and the [team](/teams) libraries you created or joined. In ordinary Web Studio
use, source records and files go directly between your browser and Apple. The
optional always-on MCP connection is the exception described below.

The library switcher works as on the Mac: click your name at the bottom of the
rail and choose **Library**. Web Studio can switch to a team, read its sources,
notebooks, Pages and shared transcripts, and work the task board of a team
notebook. A notebook's **Share** (below) gives its
team's link or has your Mac share it; inviting people by name and removing
members, and sharing anything else into a team, happen on the Mac in this
release.

Press **⌘K** or **⇧⌘P** (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) for the command menu, as on
the Mac and in Codex: one field, "Search chats or run a command". Before you
type it lists your pinned and recent chats and your recent Pages; as you type
it finds places (the Space, Pages, Sites, Courses, Google Drive, Plugins),
chats, notebooks, Pages and Sites, and sources by title. ↑ and ↓ move, Return
opens, Escape closes. Inside a Page, ⌘K still makes a link.

The web app opens on your Space, as the Mac does: **All** lists every notebook
and course beside your Pages, sites and Google Docs, and the Space's search finds
a notebook by its name, summary or topic. **New ▾ › Notebook** asks for nothing:
the notebook opens at once as **New notebook**, and once it holds a source or a
chat, ForeverLM on your Mac names it. A name you type is never replaced. A
notebook's **⋯** has Share…, **Add to Collection** (tick a collection, or make a
new one), **Pin to Sidebar**, Archive and Delete; an archived notebook waits in
**Trash** with **Restore**. With a collection made, the sort and filter menu's
**Group by Collection** shows All under each collection's heading, whose **⋯**
renames or deletes it; this browser remembers the choice. **Courses**, in the
Space's sidebar, shows every course, and a course opens its lessons there. The
Space's lists come newest activity first, a page's **⋯** leads with Codex's
**Rename…**, **Pin** and **Move to Trash**, and a pinned page dragged onto
another in **Pinned** moves there, the same order your Mac shows. ⌘-click (Ctrl
on Windows) or Shift-click rows to choose several, and the bar at the foot of
the list moves them to a space, pins them or puts them in Trash (in Trash,
restores or deletes them). The search in All, Pages and a space finds a page
by what it says as well as by its name; a page this browser has not read yet
is fetched for the search in the background. A space's **⋯** moves it to
**Trash**, where **Restore** brings it back with its pages; Trash keeps
everything until you empty it. **New ▾** also makes an **Image** (a new chat
with "Create an image of" typed) and a page from one of the learning
templates, as on the Mac. The
rail's **Notebooks** comes back to All, and under it are your pinned notebooks
and the six you worked in last, rather than every notebook. A chat's right-click
has **Pin Chat**, **Rename**, **Mark as Unread**, Move to Notebook and **Delete**
(which asks first): a pinned chat leaves the Chats list for **Pinned**, under
your pinned notebooks, the same pins your Mac shows, and dragging one onto
another reorders them. A chat whose reply finished while you had another chat
open shows a dot, as does a notebook holding one, until you open it; these
dots are this browser's own. **Memory** starts a
review of what is due in a chat; **Plugins** lists the plugins a browser can
reach (Google Drive, Slack, Linear, GitHub and Notion) with whether each is
connected, and a plugin's page has its one action: **Manage** for the four read
on the web, **Open Mac App** for Google Drive, whose folders are chosen on the
Mac. Settings has no Plugins, as on the Mac. A notebook opens as one
page with three columns: **Sources** (tick the ones its chats read; a new chat
reads the ticked sources, and a source you add arrives ticked, even one you
unticked before removing it; each notebook it uses context from is a group
below its own sources, whose box puts that notebook's sources into the chats
or takes them out, and **Add a Notebook to Context…** in **Add sources** links
another notebook and puts it in at once), **Chat**, and **Studio**; on a window narrower than
800 pixels they are three tabs, one at a time. A source you open, from
Sources or a citation in the chat, opens in the full Reader (its PDF, page,
video or transcript, with your highlights) beside the chat, in Studio's place;
**×** goes back to Studio. The search at the top of
Sources looks in your library as you type, the notebook's own sources first,
then the rest of your library, where a click reads a source without adding it
and **＋** (or **＋ Add to notebook** in the Reader) adds it. Choose **Web**, or press Return, and your Mac searches the
web for what you typed; the results arrive as a card in Sources, and one your
library already holds is added from your library, never imported twice.
**Add sources** takes a pasted link or DOI, or files from your computer.
Studio lists what the notebook made, each with its kind, how many sources it
was made from and how long ago: its Pages open in the column to read and edit,
and **＋ Page** starts one in the notebook; an audio overview shows its recipe
and script, a walkthrough what it teaches, an explainer its script and a
course (one the notebook follows) its lectures, and they play in the Mac or iPhone app. The notebook's
name, or its **⋯**, opens **Notebook settings**: its name and instructions,
research questions and the rest of its card.
**Share**, at the top right, shows who has the notebook in each team it is in
and gives **Copy Invitation Link**, the link that lets people join that team
and so get the notebook with its sources. A notebook in no team is shared by
your Mac while it is open: it makes a team named after the notebook, moves the
notebook into it and answers with the link. The first time you share, on any device, you confirm
that you have the rights to share these sources.
Studio's **Cards** reviews the notebook's kept cards one at a time: type
your answer and press **Check** (Return), or **I don't know**; ForeverLM checks
your answer against the card with your Card Answer Check model and says
**Right**, **Partly** or **Not quite** with a line on what was missing. The
check's grade is the dark button and Return takes it; the other says **I was
right** or **I was wrong**, and what you choose is recorded, counted the way
the Mac counts it. When the check cannot run, the card says so and you grade
it yourself. It
brings in new cards up to your **New cards each day** (Settings → General →
Memory review on your Mac, 10 by default), counting those you began today in
any notebook; the rest wait for another day, or for **Practice** once nothing
is left for today.

A **mnemonic reading** your Mac made opens from the notebook's Studio: each
chapter's own text with a question after the passage it tests. Answer in your
head, **Show Answer**, then **Forgot** or **Remembered**; either keeps the
question as a card, as on your Mac, and it comes back in the card review on its
schedule. An answered question folds to one line saying when it comes back, and
**Next Question** goes to the next one you have not answered.

Studio makes things too. **Report** writes a briefing, a study guide,
questions for a meeting, feedback on a document or a report of your own into a
Page in the notebook, here in a notebook chat or on your Mac. Written here, each
source is read whole when it fits: 24,000 characters a source, 32,000 in all.
When a source is longer, the dialog names it and offers **Write on your Mac**,
which reads every source whole; a report never reads part of a source. **Deep research** opens a chat that
searches the web. A notebook's empty chat has a **Feynman Technique** chip that asks you to say what you know about the notebook, from memory, then asks about the gaps and fills them in, as on your Mac (see [Inside a notebook](/studio#inside-a-notebook)); reopened on either, it goes on asking. **Audio overview**, **Make cards**, **Mnemonic reading**,
**Course** and **Explainer**, a source's **Walk through on your Mac**, and the Sources
search's **By meaning** and **Web** are done by ForeverLM on your
Mac while it is open and signed in to the same iCloud account: a paid one
shows its price first, and what it makes arrives in the notebook through
iCloud. With your Mac away, those buttons say so and wait.

The workspace around the Space holds your chats, the Reader and your
sources, two side by side at most. The search over your sources reads titles,
authors and links as you type; with words in it, **Search by meaning** (your
Mac) and **Search inside sources** look deeper, and what they find leads the
list. Books, courses, meeting series and YouTube
playlists appear among your sources as collections you expand, with their chapters and
lectures in reading order. Planning your day and scheduling sources happen in
the Mac app.

Chats use the same synced Chat model setting. The browser never holds your
OpenRouter key, so on the web they run on ForeverLM AI: they need a complimentary
account or a ForeverLM balance bought before prepaid balance was retired on
September 25, 2026; otherwise the composer says to chat in ForeverLM on your Mac.
The **+** at the left of the composer also offers Deep Research, on the chat's own model, with
live web search and cited results. Citations into a video, podcast, or
recording show the cited moment (`[2 · 12:34]`) and list it as a chip under the
source, as on the Mac; a slide frame captured from a video lives only on the Mac
that captured it, so Web Studio marks such a picture unavailable.
The web library has no voice conversation. The Chat composer's microphone
dictates: it records until you stop, for up to five minutes, then OpenRouter
transcribes the recording on the Dictation model chosen on your Mac and the
words land in the composer for you to send. The Reader's **Listen** reads a
source aloud, and the pictures Chat draws come from the model in **Settings**
-> **Images**, which lists every image model OpenRouter serves. All three go
through OpenRouter, as on the Mac, and are billed like the web's chat.

Reader progress, read state, notebook membership, and a notebook's ticks sync
back through CloudKit. Add a URL or import a PDF, text, Markdown, HTML, RTF, or
CSV file. Sources can be selected in batches, dragged onto a notebook, edited,
hidden, removed, or restored from Hidden Items, where clicking a hidden source opens
it in the Reader and it stays hidden (a hidden book is listed as its chapters).
Collection actions
cover books, courses, meeting groups, and YouTube playlists. Web-authored chats
and completed reviews also appear in the native chat history.
Notebook open questions can be added, resolved, and reopened on either Mac or
web.

The web library includes synced source metadata and readable text, and the
original PDF or EPUB of a paper, book or course file once your Mac has uploaded
it to iCloud.

Global artifact titles, notebook links, and bounded Markdown bodies sync to Web
Studio. Notepad can edit those bodies and open the existing Chat beside a
selection, carrying the passage and linked sources with it just like the Mac.
A notebook's own workspace files remain Mac-only. Search by meaning uses the Mac's on-device
embedding index, asked while the Mac is open; search by title, author and
link works here without it.

The web app does not host MCP itself: your Mac does while it is open. The
**MCP** control in Chat sets up an always-on connection. It opens a separate
iCloud authorization page so ForeverLM's hosted MCP service receives its own
revocable session instead of borrowing Web Studio's rotating session. After
**MCP ✓** appears, ChatGPT and other connected assistants can use the library
while the Mac app, iPhone app, and browser are all closed. The hosted service
keeps an account-scoped metadata and relationship cache synchronized from your
private iCloud library. It fetches readable source text from iCloud
only when a tool asks for that source and does not cache source files. Reopen
the setup page to disconnect and delete the hosted session and cache.

## In ForeverLM's new iCloud store

The web library reads and writes your library in the
[new iCloud store](/icloud-storage#foreverlms-new-icloud-store) only, the same records
as the Mac and the iPhone. A library your Mac is still uploading opens with what has
arrived so far.

- **Search inside your sources works without your Mac.** **Search inside
  sources**, under the sources' search, finds a word in the text of every source. The first time, the page asks
  whether ForeverLM's hosted assistant connection may link to your iCloud library
  to build the index; say no and it asks your Mac, or, if your Mac is closed,
  searches titles and summaries only. If you use Advanced Data Protection, Apple
  blocks web access to your iCloud data, and the page says so.
- **Papers of any size are here.** Each file is stored in parts, so a large PDF is
  in the web library like a small one.
- **Team notebooks are yours to edit.** A team's notebooks, Pages and chats appear
  as before, and a notebook that was moved into a team is edited in place. Moving a
  notebook into a team from the web asks your Mac to do it.
- **Your own place is private.** Where you got to, your highlights and your cards
  are saved for you alone, as on the Mac.

## Teams

The library switcher, **Library** in the account menu at the bottom of the left
navigation, lists My Library and every team you own or have joined. Web Studio
reads a team the same way the Mac does: a team is one CloudKit zone, read from
your private database when you created it and from the shared database when you
were invited, so sources,
notebooks, Pages, and shared chats appear in that team's library. Chats shared
into a team are read-only transcripts. Every notebook's settings carry a task
board; in a team notebook a task can be assigned to a member, and members with
read-only access see the library and the board without edit controls.

A notebook's **Share** gives its team's invitation link, or asks your Mac to
share it into a new team named after it. Inviting people by name, removing
members, and sharing anything else into a team happen in the Mac app;
CloudKit's web framework has no zone-wide sharing sheet.

The first load may take longer for a large library. The browser keeps an
account-scoped local mirror and requests only CloudKit changes on later visits.
Use the refresh button to sync immediately; returning to the tab also refreshes
after a short cooldown.
