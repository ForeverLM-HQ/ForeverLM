# ForeverLM vs LiquidText

LiquidText is a deep-reading workspace for pulling excerpts out of PDFs and web pages, arranging them spatially, linking ideas, comparing distant passages, and collaborating around documents. ForeverLM is less visual and less annotation-heavy. Its strength is keeping many source types in one library and turning them into a repeatable workflow of reading, listening, conversation, scheduling, and review.

## At a glance

| | ForeverLM | LiquidText |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Free app and free over MCP; built-in AI needs a plan ($4/mo, or $19/yr for early believers) and your own OpenRouter key | Free Basic; paid purchases and Live subscriptions vary by platform and region |
| **Best for** | Long-term research library and learning | Close reading, excerpting, and spatial synthesis |
| **Sources** | Papers, articles, books, podcasts, videos, courses, meetings, and more | PDFs, documents, web pages, and reference-manager imports |
| **Annotation** | Reader, extracted text and figures, notes, and source chat | Rich ink, highlights, margin notes, excerpt workspaces, and live links |
| **Visual thinking** | Notebooks and relationships, without a canvas | Flexible workspace for arranging and connecting excerpts |
| **Collaboration** | AI assistants can operate the library; human collaboration is limited | Near-real-time shared notebooks and comments on paid plans |
| **Review** | Scheduled review dialogue with saved history | Active reading and synthesis, without the same review loop |

*LiquidText features checked against its [official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/liquidtext/id922765270) in August 2026. Its purchase catalog is complex and regional, so check the current in-app offer.*

## Where LiquidText is better

- **Deep PDF interaction.** Pinch, collapse, compare, excerpt, and annotate dense documents in ways a conventional reader cannot match.
- **Spatial synthesis.** Arrange evidence from multiple documents on a workspace and draw links between it.
- **Handwriting.** Apple Pencil and freeform ink are central, not secondary.
- **Traceability.** Tapping an excerpt returns to its original context, which is excellent for close reading and legal or academic evidence work.
- **Human collaboration.** Shared workspaces support comments and near-real-time co-editing.

## Where ForeverLM is better

- **The library goes beyond documents.** Podcasts, videos, books, courses, and meetings belong beside PDFs.
- **Source capture is broader.** Reading lists, podcast follows, plugins, URLs, files, and MCP assistants can feed the same system.
- **AI can compare complete sources.** Discuss a notebook without first extracting every relevant passage to a canvas.
- **Listening is built in.** Read papers and articles aloud while keeping playback connected to the source.
- **The workflow closes with review.** Schedule a source, explain it back, and preserve the assessment.

## Which should you choose?

Choose **LiquidText** if your work depends on close PDF annotation, handwriting, visual evidence arrangement, and passage-level comparison.

Choose **ForeverLM** if you need one private library for every source format and want AI-assisted learning to continue after reading.

The combination is natural: synthesize difficult documents in LiquidText, then retain the source set and review it in ForeverLM.
